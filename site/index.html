Where Congress stands on Ukraine

120th Congress, taking office January 3, 2027, as elected on November 3, 2026 (from Wikipedia's member list). Returning members keep their record and score. New members have no congressional voting record yet and are shown in light grey, unless Track Ukraine has published a rating card for them: switch to Track Ukraine coloring to see those. Factions show returning members only, since caucuses re-form after January 3.

Every seat in the House and Senate, colored by the member's record on Ukraine: votes on aid, sanctions and oversight, sponsored bills, and researcher notes. Scores run from −1 (strongly opposed) to +1 (strongly supportive). Click a seat, a state or a name to see the record.

Track Ukraine mode shows only Track Ukraine's own verdicts from their Instagram rating cards: green “Supports Ukraine”, yellow “Unclear on Ukraine”, red “Opposes Ukraine”. Grey seats have no card yet. Cards are collected from Track Ukraine's site feed, which shows only their latest posts, so coverage grows with each refresh. Their verdicts can differ from the vote-based score: Steil and Huizenga get red cards despite a mostly supportive voting record.

Scores use the same formula as Track Ukraine's tracker: roll-call votes weighted by importance, sponsorship (×1.5) and researcher notes, pulled toward the party average for members with a short record. Members with fewer than 2 scored actions show as No record. One correction to Track Ukraine's data: for H.R. 4365 (FY24 defense budget) we score the Gaetz amendment, roll call 438, which is the vote their own note names, instead of the final-passage votes their data uses. Because of this, scores differ slightly from trackukraine.com (e.g. Massie −0.76 there, −0.82 here).
